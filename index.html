What stands out quickly

Digital familiarity is already present, daily medicine routines still break most often around timing, households lean toward practical rather than highly complex product concepts, and demand is strongest when pricing stays grounded.

Urban context

Most responses sit in Tier 2 cities, so the strongest opportunity looks practical and urban-growing rather than only metro-premium.

Digital comfort

Smartphone use is already visible in 76% of caregiver-reported elder answers, which supports light digital assistance instead of a fully manual-only approach.

Commercial signal

Portable organiser interest is strongest at 39%, and 77% stay at Rs 2,000 or below, so simplicity plus price discipline matters.

Overall insights and design direction

Key insights

Medicine management today is still shaped by routine, memory, and manual storage habits more than by structured support systems. Digital familiarity exists, but it is uneven and should be treated as an enabler, not an assumption.

What matters most

Timing support is the strongest opportunity area. Forgetting when to take medicines, not just where they are stored, appears as the most persistent day-to-day problem across the study.

Preferred product direction

The strongest pull is toward practical, portable, easy-to-understand formats rather than highly robotic or overly complex solutions. The product should feel familiar before it feels advanced.

Design principles

Keep the interaction simple, support reminder flexibility, fit existing household storage behavior, use voice where natural, and stay within accessible price expectations if adoption is the goal.
